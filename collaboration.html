Collaboration

High-impact partnerships addressing sustainability challenges

Overview

FFP's multi-stakeholder approach delivers a trusted, collaborative space where financial institutions, regulators, policymakers, scientists, and civil society learn from peers, gain sharper insights into evolving environmental risks, catalysing action to support the transition to a more sustainable and inclusive financial system.

Believe finance must not be neutral. Systemic change cannot be achieved alone.

Pakistan's financial sector faces mounting pressure to reckon with environmental and social risks from climate change, biodiversity loss and pollution. FFP forges coalitions of the willing, navigating the dynamic intersection where science, regulation, standards and sustainable innovation meet to translate scientific evidence into decision-useful financial methodologies and move beyond declaratory commitments toward capital allocation reform.

In recent years, FFP has expanded its policy engagement around clean air, industrial transition, and sustainable finance, including the ASIC Pakistan Mitigation Series 2025 and the development of the Pakistan Clean Air Finance Framework (PCAFF), which brings together science, finance, policy, and industry to address air-pollution risks and transition pathways.

Our Approach

Research Partnership with the University of California (UC) Davis Air Quality Research Center anchors the ASIC Pakistan Mitigation Series in world-class scientific expertise, facilitating the transfer of global knowledge to deploy next-generation air sensors and industrial emissions-control technologies across Pakistan's 11 high emissions sectors.

Our Institutional Alliances with the Rawalpindi Chamber of Commerce and Industry (RCCI) and leading civil society organizations including the Center for Peace and Development Initiatives, TheNetwork for Consumer Protection and Indus Consortium, we leverage the Pakistan Clean Air Finance Framework (PCAFF) to turn scientific data into bankable projects, ensuring that advanced mitigation technologies are not only accessible but financially viable for large-scale industrial adoption.

Collaboration pathways with a national coalition of think tanks, business chambers, environmental bodies, consumer protection groups, and parliamentary institutions builds cross-sector evidence-driven partnerships to drive collective impact on Pakistan's sustainability agenda at scale.

As a member of Fair Finance Asia, a network of over ninety civil society organisations across ten countries, and Fair Finance International, a network operating across twenty-four countries, FFP's initiatives remain internationally comparable to global investors, policymakers, and multilateral institutions.
