ASIC Pakistan Mitigation Series 2025

A science-policy-finance platform advancing clean air pathways

Convened By Fair Finance Pakistan & UC Davis AQRC
Location Islamabad, Pakistan
Series Dates 9-10 December 2025
Governance Model Quadruple Helix
Overview Institutional Partnerships Conference & Dialogue Platforms Knowledge Outputs & Policy Engagement Future Roadmap Contact

Overview

The ASIC Pakistan Mitigation Series 2025 represents Pakistan's first science-policy-finance convergence platform for air quality and industrial transition. Held in Islamabad on 9-10 December 2025, it was a first-of-its-kind Global South convening, repositioning air pollution from an environmental concern to a material financial and systemic risk with direct consequences for economic productivity, portfolio stability, and public health.

Organized by Fair Finance Pakistan in collaboration with the Air Quality Research Center (AQRC) of the University of California, Davis, one of the world's leading institutes on atmospheric science, the Series brought together financial institutions, government ministries, central banks, multilateral lenders, scientists, legal scholars, and civil society. Its central proposition: that air pollution is not only measurable and manageable, but financeable, if addressed systemically.

"The ASIC Pakistan Mitigation Series was designed to explore actionable solutions aimed at accelerating nationally owned clean-air and industrial-transition pathways, where science guides policy, finance supports transition, and industry leads innovation."

Asim Jaffry, Founder, ASIC Pakistan Mitigation Series & Country Program Lead, Fair Finance Pakistan

This Mitigation Series marks the first pilot focused on mitigation in countries where air pollution is the leading environmental and public health threat. It leverages data-driven solutions to drive policy reform, innovative finance, and regional collaboration around breakthrough technologies, delivering three interconnected wins.

+ Fewer premature deaths & better health outcomes
+ Progress on climate & air pollution mitigation goals
+ Economic resilience & competitiveness

The Scale of Pakistan's Air Pollution Crisis

Air pollution is one of Pakistan's most pressing development challenges. PM2.5 levels routinely exceed safe limits and pollution-related disease costs the economy an estimated 6.5% of GDP annually. Air pollution causes premature deaths every year from chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, strokes, and asthma, and shortens average life expectancy by more than five years in most polluted regions of Pakistan.

The Quadruple Helix Governance Model

The Series applies a Quadruple Helix Governance Model, positioning civil society as a catalytic fourth pillar alongside academia, policy, and industry. This structure enables the platform to move beyond declaratory commitments toward capital allocation reform, translating scientific evidence into decision-useful financial methodologies and enforceable policy frameworks.

Institutional Partnerships

A collaborative network spanning finance, science, industry, policy, law, and civil society.

The ASIC Pakistan Mitigation Series 2025 was built on a foundation of cross-sectoral institutional engagement, bringing together a diverse range of partners who collectively represent the policy, financial, scientific, industry leadership, civil society and community dimensions required for systemic clean-air action.

Co-Organizing Partners

  • Fair Finance Pakistan — Lead convener and platform host; member of Fair Finance Asia and Fair Finance International. FFP's mission is to strengthen financial institutions' commitment to social, environmental, and human rights standards.
  • UC Davis Air Quality Research Center (AQRC) — Co-organizer and scientific anchor; globally recognized leader in atmospheric science, air quality monitoring, and policy-relevant research. AQRC contributed advanced scientific and policy recommendations enabling evidence-based strategies for low-emission industrial growth and improved public health outcomes.

Multilateral and Development Finance Institutions

  • Asian Development Bank (ADB) — Contributed international evidence on clean-air finance mechanisms, industrial transition, and the macroeconomic case for action, drawing on China's landmark 44% PM2.5 reduction experience.
  • UN Environment Programme Finance Initiative (UNEP FI) — Chaired the plenary on finance sector pathways for industrial transition, exploring operationalization of responsible banking principles within Pakistan's financial system.

Diplomatic Partners

  • Embassy of Sweden to Pakistan — Keynote participation from H.E. Alexandra Berg von Linde, Ambassador of Sweden to Pakistan. Sweden's innovation-led industrial transformation provided an international model for Pakistan's export-oriented sectors navigating global value-chain expectations.

Government, Regulatory & Parliamentary Institutions

  • State Bank of Pakistan (SBP) — Engaged in discussions on operationalizing Pakistan's Green Taxonomy and integrating air-pollution outcomes into credit decisions.
  • Pakistan & Punjab Environmental Protection Agency — Scientific and regulatory participation in policy design sessions.
  • Parliamentary Standing Committee on Climate Change and Environmental Coordination — Parliamentary engagement on Article 9A constitutional protections and the right to a healthy environment.
  • Pakistan Institute for Parliamentary Services (PIPS) — Strategic policy convener bridging discussions on scientific research and financial pathways to accelerate industrial transition and clean-air action.

Industry Leadership

  • D.G. Khan Cement — Presented technically grounded pathways for industrial emissions reductions including material innovation.
  • Yunus Textiles Mills Limited — Demonstrated how vertically integrated exporters are driving industrial decarbonization in Pakistan.
  • Sialkot Chamber of Commerce and Industry — Highlighted the success of cluster-based infrastructure in the Sialkot Tannery Zone, reframing industrial pollution control as a critical public health and cancer-mitigation strategy.

Academic & Research Institutions

  • Lahore University of Management Sciences (LUMS) — Dr. Abid Burki, Professor Emeritus of Economics, chaired the Technical Program Committee, providing strategic leadership and overall direction to the ASIC Pakistan Mitigation Series.
  • Duke University — Dr. Michael Howard Bergin, Sternberg Family Professor of Civil & Environmental Engineering, co-chaired the Technical Program Committee.
  • Loyola University Maryland — Prof. Elizabeth Kennedy, Professor of Law & Social Responsibility, chaired the session on Rule of Law, Clean Air, and the Right to Life.
  • Chinese Academy of Sciences, Guangzhou Institute of Geochemistry, Beijing — Shared global expertise on industrial decarbonization to provide a scientific foundation for climate accountability and public-health governance.
  • National University of Sciences and Technology (NUST) & Government College University (GCU) Lahore — Contributed scientific expertise to translate environmental evidence into actionable regulatory and sectoral strategies aligned with international clean-air standards.

Development Partners

  • Center for Peace and Development Initiatives — Reframed industrial decarbonization as a rights-based issue to safeguard the public’s constitutional right to life.
  • TheNetwork for Consumer Protection — Championed a consumer-centric perspective,calling for equitable and affordable industrial transition pathways protecting citizens from the externalized costs of industrial pollution.
  • Doaba Foundation & Community Development Concern — Brought civil society perspectives on localized enforcement, municipal budgets, and community participation to bridge the divide between national constitutional rights and ground-level

International Civil Society Networks

  • Fair Finance Asia (FFA) — Regional network partner; brought forward Asia's perspective on transitional minerals, climate finance, and air pollution, emphasizing just-transition principles for critical mineral supply chains.
  • Fair Finance International (FFI) — Global network partner; contributed the Fair Finance Guide International Methodology, a robust benchmarking tool for holding financial institutions to account for environmental and community impacts.
  • ARUP — Provided critical framing for rethinking urban transport systems through breakthrough mitigation strategies.
  • GIZ Pakistan — Sustainability podium speaker presenting the World Bank-supported Punjab Clean Air Program and its data-driven, enforcement-led approach.

Conference & Dialogue Platforms

Structured dialogue designed to move from scientific evidence to implementable commitments.

UC Davis AQRC-convened ASIC Conferences are the leading global forum for experts tackling air quality, climate change, and environmental sustainability. These events bring together scientists, policymakers, and industry leaders to explore cutting-edge air sensor technologies, real-world applications, and data-driven solutions that improve public health and environmental justice. Attendees gain hands-on experience with next-generation monitoring tools, connect with key stakeholders, and explore scalable innovations tailored for low- and middle-income countries.

The ASIC Pakistan Mitigation Series 2025 hosted 12 thematic plenaries including 5 academic sessions over two days, each designed to translate scientific evidence into institutional commitments, uniting advanced research with policy leadership to pioneer breakthrough approaches across 11 high-emission sectors and industrial-urban hotspots and address urgent climate and pollution challenges head-on.

Plenary Sessions

Plenary I

Keynotes: Clean Air Policy and Finance Pathways

Anchored Pakistan's clean-air agenda within a constitutional and economic framework, framing air quality as a fundamental Right to Life under Article 9A. Featuring H.E. Alexandra Berg von Linde and Nissanka Salgado of the Asian Development Bank, the session aligned industrial competitiveness with just-transition principles and multilateral climate finance.

Plenary II

From Emission Sources to Clean Air: Circular Economy Pathways to Measurable Air Quality Gains in Pakistan

Chaired by , Director of UC Davis AQRC, the session addressed Pakistan's gaps in linking emissions to ambient air improvements, covering regulatory design, reference-grade monitoring, and auditable data systems aligned with national goals and international clean air standards.

Plenary III

Energy Transition & Industrial Decarbonization Pathways for Clean Air

Addressed Pakistan's fragmented clean-air governance, convening industry, finance, and civil society. Industry representatives from textiles and cement demonstrated that decarbonization is increasingly a competitiveness strategy, framing clean air as inseparable from export competitiveness, energy security, and constitutional rights.

Plenary IV

Financing Clean Air: Finance Sector Pathways for Industrial Transition

Chaired by UNEP FI and PIPS, with participation from commercial banks, capital markets, and the State Bank of Pakistan. Explored operationalizing SBP's Green Taxonomy, sustainability-linked lending, and integrating air-pollution outcomes into credit decisions and financial portfolios.Read more

Plenary V

Launch: Policy Brief "Air Pollution is a Solvable Problem"

Fair Finance Pakistan launched its policy brief, developed with UC Davis AQRC, providing a rigorous assessment of Pakistan's National Clean Air Policy and Punjab Clean Air Policy, while advancing targeted interventions, finance-sector strategies, and NDC alignment.

Plenary VI

Rule of Law, Clean Air & Right to Life: Climate Accountability for Executives and the State

Chaired by Professor Elizabeth Kennedy, this session examined Pakistan's Article 9A constitutional right to a healthy environment, public-interest litigation, and the emerging corporate fiduciary obligation to address air pollution risk.

Plenary VII

Transitional Minerals, Climate Finance & Responsible Supply Chains

Convened by Fair Finance Pakistan with Fair Finance Asia and Fair Finance International, examining Pakistan's role in global clean-energy supply chains and ensuring rising Global North demand for critical minerals integrates just-transition principles.

"Measuring the chemical composition of PM2.5 and making that data public is essential. Without it, neither policymakers nor financial institutions can accurately assess exposure, attribute sources, or evaluate transition pathways."

, Director, UC Davis Air Quality Research Center

Academic Sessions

Five academic sessions featuring 22 scientific papers from 15+ universities, research institutions, media houses, and civil society organizations ran in parallel, covering:

  • Sensor-driven air quality monitoring and low-cost sensor calibration.
  • Chemical source apportionment of urban PM2.5, identifying major local contributors.
  • Agricultural emissions, crop-residue management, and rural-to-urban pollution dynamics.
  • Clean mobility pathways and transport sector transition.
  • Just-transition frameworks, social equity, and community-level impact assessment.

Knowledge Outputs & Policy Engagement

Translating structured dialogue into durable policy instruments and financial accountability frameworks.

The ASIC Pakistan Mitigation Series 2025 was designed from the outset to generate actionable knowledge outputs, bridging the gap between scientific evidence, financial action, and legislative reform. Outputs span policy publications, financial sector engagement, and international media coverage.

Policy Publications

Policy Brief: "Air Pollution is a Solvable Problem"

Launched at the Series and developed with UC Davis AQRC. Provides a rigorous assessment of Pakistan's National Clean Air Policy and Punjab Clean Air Policy, advancing targeted policy interventions, finance-sector engagement strategies, and NDC 3.0 alignment.

White Paper on Clean Air & Industrial Transition

Negotiated solutions from the Series are intended to directly inform this flagship document for submission to the Parliament of Pakistan, supporting Pakistan's first cross-institutional, finance-backed framework to address air pollution.

Financial Sector Engagement

  • Discussions on operationalizing the State Bank of Pakistan's Green Taxonomy, moving from classification to active portfolio alignment.
  • Exploration of sustainability-linked lending and clean-air bond structures, referencing China's Clean Air Bond model aligned with Climate Bonds Initiative and MDB standards as a template for Pakistan.
  • Assessment of blended finance and transition finance mechanisms to de-risk investments across 11 high-emission heavy industries.
  • Application of the Fair Finance Guide International Methodology as a benchmarking tool to assess financial institutions on climate change, nature, biodiversity and human-rights considerations, underscoring the urgency of portfolio-aligned accountability over transaction-level compliance.

"A banking system that remains neutral in the face of 128,000 annual premature deaths and PM2.5 levels routinely exceeding safe limits is not neutral, it is mispricing risk. Modern finance must confront this truth. Repurposing finance is the key for our times."

Future Roadmap

Priorities

  • Development of a Pakistan Clean Air Finance Framework, linking transition finance instruments to measurable air quality and health outcomes.
  • Deepened engagement with high-emission industries including cement, textiles, and energy-intensive SMEs on commercially viable, finance-enabled transition pathways.

Long-term vision

  • Financial institutions pricing air pollution and climate risk across portfolios, not just individual transactions.
  • Industrial sectors leading decarbonization as a competitiveness strategy, not a regulatory obligation.
  • Pakistan's constitutional right to a healthy environment under Article 9A translated into enforceable obligations and measurable public health outcomes.

"Ultimately, the ASIC Pakistan Mitigation Series demonstrates that clean air is achievable. When finance is aligned with science and public interest, it becomes a pathway to tangible outcomes: lower systemic risk, healthier communities, resilient industries, and, quite literally, the possibility of breathing clean air again."

From Dialogue to Financial Architecture, Fair Finance Pakistan, 2026

As air pollution increasingly threatens development gains across South Asia and the broader Global South, the ASIC Pakistan Mitigation Series offers a potential model for how countries can move from siloed debate to coordinated, finance-enabled action. The Series will continue as an annual platform, uniting advanced research with policy leadership, and engaging international research institutions, policymakers, financial institutions, and industry to pioneer approaches that deliver triple wins: fewer premature deaths, better health outcomes, and progress on climate and economic goals.
